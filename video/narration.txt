Meet Aevum Pay — escrow invoice payments with agent spend caps. My entry for the Aevum Builder Challenge number one, on the Aevum testnet.
Paying in stablecoin today is trust on a prayer. You send the money first and hope the work arrives — or you hand funds to an AI agent and hope it does not overspend. Aevum Pay fixes both with one contract. The payer locks t U S D 8 in escrow until delivery is confirmed. And an agent can pay only up to a cap the payer set — the agent never holds the payer's funds.
The contract is Aevum Pay Escrow, written in Solidity with Foundry for the Shanghai E V M. It holds t U S D 8, six decimals. Nine functions do the work: create, fund, and confirm an invoice, refund it, claim it when it expires, authorize and revoke an agent, fund an invoice as an agent, and register a human-readable name.
It is tested, not just written. Forty-one unit tests pass, zero fail. Six fork tests against the real t U S D 8 pass, zero fail. And the end-to-end run on a local fork of Aevum: invoice zero released, invoice one released and funded by the agent, invoice two refunded, invoice three expired — every lifecycle path verified on chain.
Here is the agent, live. The agent address is on screen. Invoice number four: ten t U S D 8, status created, memo agent demo invoice. The payer set a cap of fifty t U S D 8, spent zero, remaining fifty. The agent sends the transaction — and invoice four is funded. Ten t U S D 8 now held in escrow.
Checked on chain after: invoice four is funded, the escrow holds ten t U S D 8, and the agent's remaining cap is forty. The money moved from the payer, through escrow — never through the agent's wallet.
The frontend is a static page — ethers version six plus MetaMask. Create and fund invoices, confirm delivery, refund, claim expired ones, authorize and revoke agents, and register names. No build step, no framework, no private keys in the page.
One honest blocker. The Aevum testnet deployment is waiting on the faucet. The fresh deployer wallet holds zero t Q DAY, and the web faucet needs a browser wallet, a bot check, and a mainnet balance the fresh wallet does not have. Everything else is done and tested on the fork — the testnet deploy is one funded wallet away.
